# Accuracy

How closely tukey's results match SciPy, MATLAB and the exact answer.

## In short

- Most functions agree with SciPy and MATLAB to within a few units in the last place (ULP) of a float64, about 1e-15.
- Where SciPy's own answer is measurably off, tukey is compared against the exact answer instead, and matches it.
- The larger differences come from filters whose poles sit very close to z = 1, where SciPy and MATLAB differ from each other by as much.
- Every figure here is re-measured by `just tolerances`, and CI tests will fail if any grow past set tolerance.

## How it's measured

- Reference vectors are generated by SciPy 1.18.1, then the same inputs are run through MATLAB R2025a.
  They're committed in `testdata/`, so tests can be run on a system without python or matlab.
- Each figure is the worst error over every vector, relative to the largest expected value, or for filters the largest input.
- It's the worst on either amd64 or arm64, which round slightly differently, arm64 fuses multiply-adds.
- For scale, 1e-16 is about 1 ULP.
- Each test's tolerance is about 10 times the worst figure.

A `*` marks a comparison against the exact answer, computed with mpmath at 50 digits or in rational arithmetic and rounded once,
where SciPy's own answer is off or SciPy has no equivalent.

## The difficult filters

One test filter, `butter(8, 0.01)`, is deliberately difficult:
its poles sit so close to z = 1 that single rounding differences between implementations are amplified.
It sets the larger figures for the sections, `SOSFreqz` and `SOSFiltFilt`, and every other filter agrees within a few ULP.

Two filters from the original MATLAB application, of order 2 at 0.001 low-pass and 0.0008 high-pass, are harder still,
so they're checked separately, with looser tolerances that don't loosen anything else.
Rounding differences on them grow by roughly the square of 1/(1 - pole).

| Function      | Against SciPy | Against MATLAB |
| ------------- | ------------- | -------------- |
| `Filter`      | 9.5e-13       | 6.2e-13        |
| `FilterState` | 9.9e-14       | 1.1e-13        |
| `FiltFilt`    | 4.4e-12       | 1.5e-11        |
| `Freqz`       | 1.5e-12       | 1.1e-11        |

SciPy and MATLAB differ from each other on these filters by as much.
At DC the 0.001 low-pass's gain is exactly 1, and `Freqz` gives 1 + 1.6e-12 where MATLAB gives 1 + 1.3e-11,
so much of the difference is MATLAB's.

## Where SciPy is off

These are why some comparisons are against the exact answer.

- `ellip` is off by 1.5e-13, `bessel` by 2.7e-14, and `firwin` by 3.0e-15.
- `iirpeak` forms its gain as 1 - 1/(1 + β), which cancels for a narrow bandwidth, off by 6.1e-13 at w0 = 0.002, Q = 30.
- The four order selections find a bandstop's edges with a numerical minimiser, good to about 1e-5.
- `group_delay` gives 0 at a zero on the unit circle, and is 3% off for `butter(8, 0.01)`.
- `savgol_filter` fits raw powers of the positions by `lstsq`, 1e-12 off at n = 21, and wrong by 21% at n = 101, degree 10.
- `medfilt` only zero-pads, and handles NaN however it happens to sort.

## Where MATLAB is off

These set the larger figures against MATLAB.

- `ellip` forms 1 - k1² for the specification's small modulus k1 and loses its digits, 9.9e-9 from the exact design.
- `iirpeak` forms its gain the same way as SciPy, 6.1e-13 off.
- `cheby1` differs from SciPy by as much as from tukey.
- `grpdelay` loses digits near poles and zeros crowded against the unit circle:
  3.2e-11 at worst on (b, a), 2.2e-5 on the two very low cutoff filters, and 5.7e-9 on `butter(8, 0.01)`'s sections.
- `xcorr` works by FFT, so it's 2.4e-4 off the exact answer on small outputs beside large ones,
  though within 6.3e-16 of the largest, the scale these comparisons use.
- `medfilt1` is 1 ULP off at some even-count midpoints near the truncated ends, and rounds a midpoint of two subnormals twice.
- `sgolayfilt` loses digits at a 101-sample window of degree 10, 2.5e-14 off.
- `sum` and the other sums are plain, so 1e8 among 20,000 thousandths, then cancelled, sums to 20.00001, where `Sum` gives 20.
- The RMS `envelope` is 4.5e-15 from the exactly summed answer, where `Envelope` is within 1e-15 of it.
- `findsignal` rounds at the scale of the sums of squares, so its perfect matches can be up to 2^-29 rather than 0.
- `spectrogram` at a frequency vector is 2.4e-13 from the exact sums, even at the FFT's own bins.

## Not compared

- Second-order sections against MATLAB, as `zp2sos` gives a first-order section one zero rather than two,
  and breaks ties in pole radius differently, so it finds the same filter in different sections.
- `Bessel` against MATLAB beyond order 25, the highest `besselap` has, as MATLAB has no digital Bessel design.
- MATLAB's order-selection edges where they follow a different convention:
  a bandstop's edges from `cheb1ord` and `ellipord`, which are returned unmoved, and a bandpass's from `cheb2ord`.
- MATLAB's `grpdelay` on `butter(8, 0.01)`'s (b, a), which is 7.3% off.
- MATLAB's `medfilt1` at a midpoint of two subnormals, and `sgolayfilt` at n = 101, degree 10, which are in a suite of their own.
- `Percentile` with NaN against MATLAB, as `prctile` ignores NaN where tukey gives NaN.

## Measuring it yourself

```sh
just tolerances
```

prints the worst error of every suite against its tolerance, on every architecture the machine can run.
